## The bet

Observed behavior convinces where product claims cannot. The page presents one historical run as a selected case record: a recognizable task, the real chronology including waste and limits, independently attested activity, and the delivered result. Everything after the record explains or sells only what the visitor has already observed.

## Ordered sections

1. **The assignment and receipt**

   **Job:** Make the work legible and desirable before asking the visitor to evaluate its provenance.

   **What the visitor sees:** A plain-language description of the job, followed by the exact original request and a recognizable preview of the delivered result. Once the task is understood, the record identifies the requester, completion date, start and delivery times, elapsed duration, model used, and demonstrated stack. It is explicitly a selected historical run, completed before 2026-08-10. An original external artifact is openable from this first section.

2. **The event record**

   **Job:** Let the visitor inspect how real work unfolded across hours, including the friction a staged demonstration would remove.

   **What the visitor sees:** One continuous chronology whose order is dictated entirely by the original timestamps. It includes, wherever they actually occurred:

   1. The starting request and permissions.
   2. The agent’s substantive actions and external tool activity.
   3. At least one unflattering dead end, abandoned approach, retry, or wait at useful resolution.
   4. A consequential permission boundary: narrow escalation, an in-scope workaround after refusal, or a genuine approval pause.
   5. A meaningful unattended interval, marked as elapsed historical time rather than simulated waiting.
   6. Delivery of the result.

3. **The result, opened**

   **Job:** Prove that the activity produced useful work rather than an impressive-looking trace.
