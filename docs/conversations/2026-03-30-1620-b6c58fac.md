## User

Yeah, that's fishy, isn't it? Why is the view not calling get_effective_tagas? Would it complicate? Or is it natural?

---

## Assistant

It's actually natural given what each consumer needs. There's a shape mismatch:

- **The ABAC engine** wants a flat merged list of `(key, value, source)` tuples — it doesn't care about the grouping, it just needs the full effective set to evaluate policies against.
- **The view** needs two separate collections: `direct_tags` as an editable set (serialized to `tags_json` for Alpine, passed to the tag editor), and `inherited_tags` as a read-only display set. The UI treats them fundamentally differently.

If the view called `get_effective_tags`, it would get a flat list of tuples and then have to split them back apart by source — parsing `"inherited:WorkspaceName"` strings to re-separate what it already had as two clean querysets. That's arguably worse.

The real risk isn't the duplication of the *query* — it's the duplication of the *inheritance rule*. If you later add environment-to-app inheritance, you'd need to remember to update both `get_effective_tags` in the ABAC service and `build_app_detail_context` in the view. That's the part that's fragile.

A clean middle ground would be a function like `get_inherited_tags(app)` that returns the queryset of inherited `ResourceTag` objects — used by the view directly for display, and called by `get_effective_tags` internally to build its flat list. That centralizes the "who inherits from whom" rule in one place without forcing the view into an awkward tuple-splitting dance.

But that's a refactor, not part of the UI task. Want me to note it somewhere, or leave it for now?
