**Scale-in = removing capacity from an Auto Scaling Group (ASG).**

In AWS ASG terms:
- **Scale-out** — launch more EC2 instances (grow the fleet).
- **Scale-in** — terminate EC2 instances (shrink the fleet).

In your case, the ASG `devopshero-default-ecs-container-instances` runs a **target-tracking policy** managed by the ECS capacity provider. It watches a CloudWatch metric (roughly: "how much of the fleet's CPU+memory is reserved by running ECS tasks"). Two alarms are attached:

- `AlarmHigh` → "reserved capacity too high, not enough headroom" → **scale-out** (launch instances).
- `AlarmLow` → "reserved capacity too low, instances are underused" → **scale-in** (terminate instances).

That's exactly what you saw:
- `16:57` AlarmHigh fired → ASG launched 2 new instances.
- `17:14` AlarmLow fired → ASG decided one of those instances was unneeded → picked `i-04d0a36f09108217b` to terminate → ECS marked it DRAINING → your hermes task on it got a clean SIGTERM and was rescheduled onto the surviving instance.
